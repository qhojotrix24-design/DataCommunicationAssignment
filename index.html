1. Fundamentals of Data Communication

Data communication is the exchange of data between a source and a receiver via some form of transmission medium such as a wire cable. For data communication to occur, the communicating devices must be part of a communication system made up of a combination of hardware and software.

The Five Core Components

  • Message: The information (data) to be communicated (text, numbers, images, audio, video).
  • Sender: The device that sends the data message (computer, workstation, telephone handset).
  • Receiver: The device that receives the message.
  • Transmission Medium: The physical path by which a message travels from sender to receiver.
  • Protocol: A set of rules that govern data communication. It represents an agreement between the communicating devices.

2. Historical Milestones & Telegraphy

Long before modern fiber optics and wireless networks, electrical data communication began with the electrical telegraph. Developed in the 1830s and 1840s by Samuel Morse and other inventors, it revolutionized long-distance communication by mapping characters into electrical pulses (dots and dashes) transmitted across metallic wires.

3. Guided and Unguided Transmission Media

Data travels from source to destination via paths categorized as either physical paths (guided) or open-air paths (unguided).

Guided Media (Wired)

  • Twisted-Pair Cable: Consists of two insulated copper wires twisted together to reduce electromagnetic interference (EMI). Used extensively in LANs (e.g., Cat6).
  • Coaxial Cable: Carries high-frequency signals and has a central core conductor surrounded by an outer shield. Commonly found in cable TV networks.
  • Fiber-Optic Cable: Uses pulses of light to transmit data through glass or plastic cores. It offers the highest bandwidth and resistance to attenuation.

Unguided Media (Wireless)

  • Radio Waves: Electromagnetic waves ranging from 3 kHz to 1 GHz. They are omnidirectional and effective for wide-area coverage.
  • Microwaves: Unidirectional high-frequency waves (1 GHz to 300 GHz) requiring line-of-sight installation between line antennas.
